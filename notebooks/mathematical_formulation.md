## 1. Operations Research: Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP)
The tactical multi-period network flow optimization models minimize the total logistics cost over a planning horizon $T$ across Distribution Centers (Hubs) $I$, Retail Destinations $J$, and Commodities $K$.

### Objective Function:
$$\min \quad Z = \sum_{t \in T} \left( \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{j \in J} \sum_{k \in K} c_{ijt} \cdot x_{ijkt} + \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{k \in K} h_{ikt} \cdot y_{ikt} + \sum_{i \in I} \sum_{k \in K} p_{ikt} \cdot z_{ikt} + \sum_{i \in I} F_i \cdot u_{it} \right)$$

*   **Decision Variables:**
    *   $x_{ijkt} \ge 0$: Quantity of product $k$ shipped from DC $i$ to Retailer $j$ in period $t$ (continuous).
    *   $y_{ikt} \ge 0$: Inventory of product $k$ stored at DC $i$ at the end of period $t$ (continuous).
    *   $z_{ikt} \ge 0$: Procurement replenishment quantity of product $k$ arriving at DC $i$ in period $t$ (continuous).
    *   $u_{it} \in \{0, 1\}$: Binary state indicating if DC $i$ is open in period $t$.

*   **Constraints:**
    1.  **Demand Satisfaction:**
        $$\sum_{i \in I} x_{ijkt} \ge \hat{d}_{jkt} \quad \forall j \in J, \forall k \in K, \forall t \in T$$
        *(Where $\hat{d}_{jkt}$ is the forecast demand generated by Scikit-learn Random Forest).*
    2.  **Inventory Flow Balance:**
        $$y_{ikt} = y_{i,k,t-1} + z_{ikt} - \sum_{j \in J} x_{ijkt} \quad \forall i \in I, \forall k \in K, \forall t \in T$$
    3.  **DC Storage Capacity Limits:**
        $$\sum_{k \in K} y_{ikt} \le C_i \cdot u_{it} \quad \forall i \in I, \forall t \in T$$
        *(Forces fixed cost $F_i$ overheads if warehouse is active).*
    4.  **Route Flow Caps:**
        $$\sum_{k \in K} x_{ijkt} \le T_{ij} \quad \forall i \in I, \forall j \in J, \forall t \in T$$

## 2. Continuous Optimization & Calculus (SymPy)
Models nonlinear holding cost penalties under storage congestion using analytical derivatives to calculate the Economic Order Quantity (EOQ).

*   **Total Annual Cost Function $C(Q)$:**
    $$C(Q) = \frac{D \cdot S}{Q} + \frac{Q \cdot H}{2} + \alpha \cdot Q^2$$
    *(where $D$ is demand, $S$ setup cost, $H$ base holding cost, and $\alpha \cdot Q^2$ the quadratic congestion penalty).*
*   **Symbolic First Derivative:**
    $$\frac{dC}{dQ} = -\frac{D \cdot S}{Q^2} + \frac{H}{2} + 2\alpha \cdot Q = 0$$
*   Solving $\frac{dC}{dQ} = 0$ for $Q^*$ yields the congestion-adjusted optimal replenishment volume.

## 3. Discrete Graph Routing (NetworkX)
The routing infrastructure is modeled as a weighted directed graph $G = (V, E)$.
*   **Graph Nodes ($V$):** Warehouses, Retailers, and Hubs.
*   **Graph Edges ($E$):** Corridors containing physical distances and active congestion factors.
*   **Algorithm:** Dijkstra's shortest-path search determines the minimum cost distance. The resulting path weight parameterizes the cost coefficient $c_{ijt}$ in the MILP solver:
    $$c_{ijt} = \text{DijkstraPathLength}(i, j) \times \text{fuel\_coefficient}$$

## 4. Vectorized Linear Algebra (NumPy)
*   **Stationary Distributions:** Analyzes supply chain bottlenecks and Markov transition probabilities $P$ by solving the system:
    $$\pi \cdot P = \pi$$
    subject to $\sum \pi_i = 1$.

## 5. Machine Learning (Scikit-Learn)
*   **Ridge Regression (L2 regularization)** and **Random Forest Regressors** predict dynamic daily demand patterns using seasonal features:
    $$\hat{y}_t = f(\text{DayOfWeek}_t, \text{Month}_t, \text{Holiday}_t, \text{Weather}_t)$$
*   **Evaluation Metrics:**
    *   Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$)
    *   Mean Absolute Error (MAE)
    *   Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)
